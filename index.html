Research

03
Honours Thesis · December 2025

Returns to Education in Canada: An Econometric Analysis of Real Wages, 2018–2025

Carleton University, Department of Economics

Using pooled Labour Force Survey (LFS) microdata across 1,759,743 observations (weighted to ~558M person-records), I estimate a fully saturated Mincer-style wage model — ln(wi) = β₁Educationi + X′iγ + δp + δt + εi — controlling for occupation, industry, union status, establishment size, family structure, province, and year, with standard errors clustered at the province level. The result: education returns in Canada are strong, monotonic, and economically large even after stripping out every observable confounder I could get my hands on.

Other findings

  • Women earn ~15.7% less than men after controlling for education, occupation, industry, region, and job characteristics — a persistent, unexplained gap consistent with prior Statistics Canada research.
  • Regional premiums are large even after conditioning on productivity-linked covariates: Alberta (+12.4%) and British Columbia (+11.1%) pay a premium; Atlantic provinces carry a −5% to −7% penalty.
  • Kernel density estimates show the wage distribution for bachelor's-degree holders isn't just shifted right of the 0–8-years group — it's also more dispersed, with a materially thicker upper tail.
